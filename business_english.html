2ReadingAbout 2 minutes

Why your English is better than it sounds

Most professionals who come to a first call already know a great deal of English. They read reports, write emails and of a fast meeting. The problem starts when it is their turn to speak. The sentence is ready in Portuguese, and it has to be rebuilt in English , while four people wait.

Three things make that moment hard, and none of them is vocabulary. The first is pronunciation: not accent, which nobody minds, but the stress and the sounds that decide whether a listener understands you the first time. The second is listening speed: native speakers in a meeting do not slow down, and if you are still processing the last sentence you cannot answer this one. The third is nerve: the habit of choosing the shorter, safer answer because it is easier to say.

All three can be trained, and they train fastest in conversation rather than in exercises. That is why every lesson starts with a text like this one: short, about something real, and then discussed. You your view, you get stuck, you get a gentle correction, and you carry on. After a few weeks the corrections become , and you find that you can in the meeting you used to quietly.

3VocabularySix expressions from the text
get the gist
Understand the main point without catching every detail. I got the gist of the call, but I missed the numbers.
on the spot
Immediately, with no time to prepare. They asked for my opinion on the spot.
put (something) across
Explain an idea so that people understand it. She put the proposal across in two minutes.
second nature
Something you do without thinking, because you have done it so often. After a month, the past-tense endings were second nature.
hold your own
Do as well as the other people in a situation. He held his own in the negotiation.
sit through (something)
Stay until the end of something long, often without taking part. I sat through the whole demo without saying a word.
Teacher's noteThese are the expressions native colleagues use without noticing. Using two of them in your next meeting will do more for how you sound than ten new nouns.
4PronunciationWhere the stress goes

Six words that Portuguese pulls the wrong way.

Portuguese stresses these words near the end. English does not. Say each one with the strong syllable loud and the others quick and quiet.

  • de·VEL·op·mentnot develop-MENT
  • MAN·age·mentnot manage-MENT
  • op·por·TU·ni·tynot opportuni-TY
  • CAT·e·go·rynot cate-GO-ry
  • IN·ter·est·ingthree syllables, not four
  • COMF·ta·blethree syllables, not four
Teacher's noteWhen the stress lands in the wrong place, a native listener often hears a different word, or no word at all, even if every sound was right. Get the stress right and most small sound mistakes stop mattering.
